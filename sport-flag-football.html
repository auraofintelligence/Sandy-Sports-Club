THE GAME

About Flag football

Flag football is non-contact American football. Instead of tackling, defenders stop the ball carrier by pulling a flag from their belt. It is fast, mixed friendly and joins the Olympic programme at Los Angeles 2028.

HOW IT'S PLAYED

How it's played

The essentials at a glance: team size, scoring, game length, the court or field, and the key rules.

Official format: IFAF 5v5 flag football. Summary only; the official rules are the source.

Team size
5 players on the field per team, with rolling substitutions.
Scoring
A touchdown is worth 6 points, followed by a try for 1 or 2 extra points from a set distance.
Match length
Two timed halves at IFAF events; local games are often shorter.
Field
50 yards by 25 yards with 10 yard end zones at each end. On sand, the field size is a club decision.
Key rules
No tackling, blocking or screening. Pulling the ball carrier's flag ends the play, and running plays are not allowed in the 5 yard no running zones before each end zone.

Source: IFAF Flag Football Rules (2026 edition, via USA Football)

JUNIORS AND YOUTH

Juniors and youth pathway

Sandy Sports is meant to be a safe, all ages club. This is a proposed pathway, not an official one.

Junior friendly format. NFL Flag is the 5 a side, non-contact school format in Australia. American Football Australia describes a pathway from school flag to community clubs, the U15 and U17 Junior Flag Nationals and national squad selection. On sand, the Sandy Sports version would be a club format, to be confirmed.

Source: American Football Australia: NFL Flag and the junior pathway
